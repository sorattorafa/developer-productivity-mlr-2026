# Past, Present, and Future of Developer Productivity Measurement: A Multivocal Literature Review

Replication package of the article.

**Authors.** Rafael Rampin Soratto (State University of Maringá, UEM, Brazil), Getúlio Coimbra (Federal University of Technology – Paraná, UTFPR, Brazil), Jorge Melegati (INESC TEC, Faculty of Engineering, University of Porto, Portugal), Igor Steinmacher (Northern Arizona University, USA), and Igor Wiese (State University of Maringá, UEM, Brazil).

**Archived release.** The DOI of the archived release will be added here after the first release on Zenodo.

## Overview

This repository holds the data, instruments, and scripts of a multivocal literature review (MLR) on how developer productivity is measured, conducted with the guidelines of Garousi et al. (2019). The review covers 2016 to 2026 and includes 228 sources, 153 academic and 75 from the gray literature.

The original searches ran in January and February 2026. An update on 3 October 2026 extended them to the end of the review window with the same strings, criteria, forms, and rubrics. Of the 228 sources, 121 come from the original search (66 academic and 55 gray) and 107 from the update (87 academic and 20 gray).

The review is a secondary study and collected no primary data. Its instruments are the extraction form, the codebook of metric categories, and the quality checklists.

## Research questions

- **RQ1.** What frameworks, metrics, and tools have been used to measure software developer productivity in academic and practitioner literature from 2016 to 2026?
- **RQ2.** How has developer productivity measurement evolved across the pre-pandemic, pandemic transition, and post-pandemic/AI era?
- **RQ3.** What is the reported impact of AI coding assistants and autonomous agents on developer productivity measurement, and what new approaches have emerged?
- **RQ4.** What are the main challenges and contextual factors that influence the effectiveness of developer productivity measurement?

## Search

All databases used the same string, adapted to the syntax of each one:

```
(("developer experience" OR "DevEx" OR "developer productivity" OR
"engineering productivity" OR "DORA metrics" OR "SPACE framework" OR
"productivity factors") AND ("framework" OR "metrics" OR "measurement"
OR "factors" OR "assessment"))
```

The sources were IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Scopus, Springer Nature, and Google Scholar (through Publish or Perish). Dates, filters, record counts, and export files per database are in `data/searches/README.md`. The raw exports of the original IEEE Xplore and Springer Nature searches were not preserved. Both searches were re-run in July 2026 with the original string, and the re-runs are dated proxies, not the original retrieval. The gray literature started from 15 practitioner outlets (framework maintainers, vendors, and independent outlets), and the update added six web queries, logged in `data/searches/grey/`.

## Extraction and use of AI

Two LLM-based assistants extracted each source independently with the same structured prompt (`extraction/prompts/`), following Felizardo et al. (2024):

- Extractor A: Claude (Anthropic). Claude Opus 4.8 through the claude.ai interface in the original search (main pass in March 2026, re-extractions from April to July 2026), and Claude Opus 5.5 in the update (October 2026). Each extraction file of the update records the model version.
- Extractor B: NotebookLM (Google), automated with `notebooklm-py[browser] >= 0.3`. The service does not disclose its underlying model.

Neither interface exposes decoding parameters, so temperature and top_p were not controlled. The first author compared both outputs with the full text and decided every field, and the extractor verdicts served only as an audit signal. In the original search, the 298 field-level discrepancies between the extractors were resolved against the full text (`extraction/comparison_report.md`). In the update, Claude also pre-classified the new records for screening, pre-filled the reading forms with the values on which both extractors agreed, and drafted the quality scores, the remaining fields, and the coding of measurement challenges, each with a quote and page from the source. The first author checked every draft against the full text and made all screening, inclusion, and exclusion decisions. Two co-authors reviewed all decisions, and disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Compliance with each recommendation of Felizardo et al. (2024) is in `protocol/FELIZARDO_COMPLIANCE_MATRIX.md`. The accuracy of the extractor verdicts against the final decisions is recomputed by `extraction/scripts/verdict_accuracy.py`.

## Quality assessment

- Academic sources (n = 153): six criteria adapted from Dybå and Dingsøyr (2008), each rated from 1 to 5; the score is the mean of the six. Mean 4.19 (SD 0.61).
- Gray sources (n = 75): six AACODS criteria from Garousi et al. (2019), each rated from 1 to 6; the score is the mean of the six. Mean 4.27 (SD 1.04).

The minimum was 3.0 on both scales.

## Reproducing the analysis

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt

# corpus counts, framework citation and use, Fisher and chi-square tests, metric categories,
# temporal distribution, sample sizes, quality means, and the vendor split
python3 extraction/scripts/verify_statistics.py      # writes data/master/mlr_stats.json

python3 extraction/scripts/metric_inventory.py       # canonical metric inventory (data/master/metric_inventory*.csv)
python3 extraction/scripts/verdict_accuracy.py       # extractor verdicts against the final decisions
python3 extraction/scripts/fig_metric_inventory.py   # figure of the most-reported metrics
python3 extraction/scripts/fig_framework_flow.py     # framework flow figure
```

Re-running the extraction stage requires the source PDFs, a Google account for NotebookLM, and browser authentication (`extraction/scripts/notebooklm/SETUP.md`).

To compile the article and the supplementary material:

```bash
cd latex-article/
pdflatex main.tex && bibtex main && pdflatex main.tex && pdflatex main.tex
pdflatex supplementary.tex && bibtex supplementary && pdflatex supplementary.tex && pdflatex supplementary.tex
```
