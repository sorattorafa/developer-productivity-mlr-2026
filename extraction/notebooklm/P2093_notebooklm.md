## Objetivo

This work aims to propose an actionable framework (PSECO-GDI) to foster the inclusion of women in software development teams within proprietary software ecosystems (PSECO) [1, 7]. Building upon a prior multivocal literature review (MLR) and a qualitative field study involving 21 female professionals from keystone organizations, the study identifies women's entry motivations, contextual characteristics influencing gender barriers, barriers faced in remaining in the ecosystem, organizational strategies, and coping mechanisms used when strategies are absent or ineffective [1, 2, 7].

---

## Questoes de Pesquisa

- **Main RQ**: Which barriers and motivations affect women’s entry and permanence in software development teams as well as contextual characteristics, strategies, and coping mechanisms present in proprietary software ecosystems? [2, 7]
- **RQ1 (FS-Q1)**: Which are women’s motivations for joining software development teams? More specifically, what are women’s motivation for joining a PSECO? [8]
- **RQ2 (FS-Q2)**: Which contextual characteristics influence in the existence and severity of barriers for women in software development teams in PSECO? [8]
- **RQ3 (FS-Q3)**: Which are the barriers that women face in software development teams in PSECO? [8]
- **RQ4 (FS-Q4)**: From your current or previous professional experience, which strategies to foster gender diversity have you seen applied to address gender barriers in PSECO? [8]
- **RQ5 (FS-Q5)**: Which coping mechanisms do women resort to when barriers occurs and the strategy to address gender barriers is not implemented? [8]

### Frameworks Propostos
- **PSECO-GDI (Proprietary Software Ecosystem — Gender Diversity and Inclusion)**: An actionable framework designed to foster women's inclusion in software development teams in proprietary software ecosystems [1, 7]. It is structured into two main pillars: **Understanding** (Motivations MO01–MO05, Contextual Characteristics CC01–CC05) and **Fostering** (Gender Barriers BA01–BA15, Organizational Strategies ST01–ST07 with 27 concrete actions Ac.01–Ac.27, Coping Mechanisms CM01–CM06), integrated with an **Ask-Plan-Act** continuous improvement operational model [1, 10, 12-16].

## Resultados Principais

- **Desenvolvimento do Framework PSECO-GDI**: Creation of a structured framework organizing 5 core components across two pillars: *Understanding* (5 Motivations, 5 Contextual Characteristics) and *Fostering* (15 Gender Barriers, 27 concrete actions across 7 Organizational Strategies, 6 Coping Mechanisms) [1, 10, 23].
- **Identificação de Duas Novas Barreiras Específicas de PSECO**: Uncovered 2 context-specific gender barriers not previously mapped in software engineering literature: *Client Resistance* (BA01 — clients refusing or demanding replacement of female developers) and *Lack of Collaboration between Business Partners* (BA06 — intentional withholding of technical information by suppliers or external partners) [1, 15, 21, 22].
- **Prevalência e Gravidade das Barreiras**: **90% of interviewees reported having experienced gender barriers** during their career in PSECO [21].
  - *Sexism* and *Imposter Syndrome* were the most mentioned barriers (18 out of 21 interviewees; 86%), followed by *Imbalance between personal and professional life* (17 interviewees; 81%) [24].
  - *Lack of peer parity* (BA07) was most frequently rated as **unbearable pain** (6 interviewees), followed by *Toxic culture*, *Glass ceiling*, and *Imposter syndrome* (3 interviewees each) [24].
  - *Prove it again* (BA11) and *Stereotypes* (BA13) were most selected as causing **intense pain** (7 interviewees each) [24].
- **Análise por Faixa Etária**:
  - Women aged 31–40 report the highest concentration of severe barriers (*Lack of peer parity*, *Work-life imbalance*, *Sexism*), coinciding with career consolidation and early maternity [24, 25].
  - *Imposter syndrome* is most painful for younger developers (20–30 and 31–40 years) and declines significantly after age 41 [25].
  - *Technical difficulties* was disproved as a major practical pain point: 15 out of 21 respondents reported experiencing no technical pain, contradicting frequent emphasis in academic literature [25, 26].
- **Mecanismos de Enfrentamento e Perda de Carreira**:
  - Gender barriers occurred with internal colleagues for 10 women (47% immediate manager, 35% peers from other areas, 18% teammates) [27, 28].
  - 32% of women who faced internal barriers **did not report the problem to anyone** [28].
  - **Over 27% of women suffered direct negative career impacts** due to unaddressed barriers (transferred areas, requested termination, or were fired) [28].
  - Power asymmetries in PSECO lead keystone organizations to tolerate client resistance and bias to preserve commercial contracts, resulting in female developer dissatisfaction [26, 29].

---

## Desafios do Uso das Metricas

- **Subnotificação e Inação Organizacional**: 32% of female developers choose not to report barriers or microaggressions due to fear of negative repercussions, lack of support, or management indifference [28].
- **Assimetria de Poder e Tolerância a Viés Comercial**: Keystone organizations prioritize commercial client relationships over developer well-being, accepting client resistance or sexist demands without corrective intervention [26, 29].
- **Penalização da Vítima (Anti-padrão Organizacional)**: Common organizational responses to reported barriers penalize the female professional (e.g., transferring her to another team, changing client assignments, or termination) rather than addressing perpetrator behavior [28, 30].
- **Normalização de Viases Sutis**: Subtle biases like "Prove it again" and exclusionary language are hard to track with formal metrics and become normalized in toxic workplace cultures [14, 15, 24].
- **Desconexão entre Literatura e Prática**: Academic literature frequently emphasizes "technical difficulties" for women, whereas empirical practice shows that female developers' primary pain points are organizational, human, and relational [25, 26].

## Limitacoes

- **Ausência de Avaliação Externa do Framework**: The proposed PSECO-GDI framework was developed empirically but has not yet been externally evaluated or validated by independent domain experts [31].
- **Falta de Prova de Conceito (Proof of Concept)**: The framework was not deployed in a long-term operational industry pilot to evaluate longitudinal effects [31].
- **Descrição em Alto Nível das Estratégias**: Organizational strategies and actions are formulated at a high level and require contextual customization by each company [31].
- **Restrição Geográfica da Amostra**: The field study sample was restricted to women working in keystone organizations operating in Brazil [31, 32].
- **Ausência de Análise Interseccional Dedicada**: The study focused primarily on gender and did not isolate intersecting identity traits such as race or sexual orientation, although age variations were evaluated [31].
- **Escopo Binário de Gênero**: The study evaluated barriers faced by self-identified women and did not encompass non-binary or other gender identities [17, 23].

---

## Contribuicao Principal

The primary contribution of this paper is the **PSECO-GDI framework**, an actionable artifact designed to assist IT managers, researchers, and keystone organizations in understanding and systematically fostering women's inclusion in proprietary software ecosystem teams [1, 12, 23]. By synthesizing findings from a multivocal literature review and a field study with 21 female professionals, the paper establishes 5 entry motivations, 5 contextual characteristics, 15 gender barriers (including 2 novel ecosystem barriers: client resistance and lack of partner collaboration), 27 concrete organizational actions across 7 strategies, and 6 coping mechanisms [1, 13-16, 28]. It further operationalizes the framework through an **Ask-Plan-Act** continuous improvement cycle tailored for industry adoption [12, 33].
