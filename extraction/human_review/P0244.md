### Evidence (quotes with section/page) — required
- "Our findings reveal a complex and at times contradictory landscape regarding the impact of AI tools on developer productivity. While tools offer support in various dimensions of SE, their effectiveness is mediated by task complexity, interaction patterns, and individual cognitive states. This tension between promised efficiency and lived experience emerges as a 'productivity paradox', a term we use to describe how AI tools can both support and disrupt developer productivity depending on context and usage patterns" — p. 7

- "Qualitative findings, however, reveal recurring inefficiencies for both tools, including repeated iterations and manual validation of outputs. This tension reflects the productivity paradox: AI feedback can accelerate work while also introducing friction that offsets its benefits." — p. 7

- "Flow State: Disruption and Support. Flow proved highly sensitive to AI tool behavior. While both Copilot and Gemini Code Assist were rated positively for supporting flow, Copilot was perceived as slightly more effective in tasks requiring deep concentration. Qualitative accounts indicate that intrusive autocompletions, context loss, and suggestion overload—particularly with Gemini, can disrupt immersion" — p. 7

- "Importantly, this paradox reflects not tool failure but socio-technical integration into existing workflows. Productivity emerges from interaction design, task characteristics, and developer expectations, suggesting that traditional metrics alone are insufficient. Future research should therefore examine not only whether AI tools improve productivity, but how, when, and for whom such gains materialize or are offset." — p. 7

- "A key insight is the productivity paradox: AI tools can both enhance and hinder productivity, depending on interaction patterns, cognitive demands, and situational conditions. This duality underscores the need for socio-technical models that avoid assuming uniformly positive effects" — p. 8
