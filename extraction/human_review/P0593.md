### Evidence (quotes with section/page) — required
- "Our findings highlight the need for methodological diversity, the integration of multi-modal data sources, and a stronger emphasis on metric validation to ensure robust and actionable results." — p. 19

- "The empirical designs employed to assess Dev-X facets vary widely in terms of method types, data types, and evaluation metrics. Surveys dominate the empirical landscape, used extensively across most facets, particularly for emotional and value-based facets like benevolence, stimulation, and achievement." — p. 17

- "developer ratings is most commonly paired with methods like surveys, and expert opinion. This is possibly because such data provides quantifiable measures for developer emotions, satisfaction, and productivity, but it should be noted that it does lack depth compared to controlled experiments, which are less adopted. Other data-method relationships include the association between source-code data and expert opinion and the association between psycho-physio data and A/B testing." — p. 10

- "Sentiments and Other Emotions are exceptional, in that they do not predominently reply on ratings/surveys. Instead, data mining and controlled experiments are the more prevalent empirical method types for these facets." — p. 10

### Free notes (if secondary study/review, note here)
- Systematic mapping of 231 studies on DevEx to understand the empirical designs, data types and metrics used to evaluate developer performance.
- By systematically categorizing 16 facets spanning emotions, values and perceptions, it identifies significant gaps in empirical coverage, such as the under-representation of facets like tradition and universalism, and an excessive reliance on survey-based classification methods.
- Highlights the need for methodological diversity, the integration of multi-modal data sources, and a stronger emphasis on metric validation to ensure robust and actionable results.
