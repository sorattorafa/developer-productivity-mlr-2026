### Evidence (quotes with section/page) — required

- "In 2022, mass layoffs occurred in Indonesia’s technology
sector, affecting over a thousand developers [1]. Three key
factors contribute to these layoffs: over-hiring during the
COVID-19 pandemic, declining investments in technology
firms, and the increasing adoption of AI [2]. Despite these
challenges, demand for skilled developers remains high [3]
and Indonesia’s technology market continues to expand [4].
The layoffs have heightened the need to improve
developer productivity, as post-layoff managers must
implement cost-saving measures while developers face
increased workloads [5]. Optimizing productivity has thus
become crucial for mitigating these challenges [6]."

- "The SPACE Framework provides a
comprehensive but complex model of software development
productivity through five dimensions: Satisfaction (S),
Performance (P), Activity (A), Communication (C), and
Efficiency (E)". This study addresses two key questions: (1) how
these dimensions interact within an input-output model, and (2)
which most strongly influence developers' perceived
productivity. Analysis of survey responses from 161 developers
using PLS-SEM reveals that performance factors, particularly
work quality, serve as the primary drivers of perceived
productivity. — Resume 

- "In contrast, quantitative activity measures show
minimal effects, and job satisfaction demonstrates no
meaningful influence. These results challenge conventional
productivity metrics that emphasize activity volume, instead
highlighting output quality as a critical indicator. Future
research should combine survey data with behavioral metrics,
develop standardized measurement approaches, and include
developers from diverse professional and cultural backgrounds."

- "Alternatively, the DevEx framework adopts a developer-
centric perspective, emphasizing experience as a productivity
driver through factors like feedback loops, cognitive load, and
flow state [22]. However, it lacks mechanisms for assessing
current productivity states. In practice, leading tech
organizations evaluate productivity through composite
measures of business impact, system performance, and
developer effectiveness [23]."

- "This study proposes defining Activity
(ACTV) as the input and Performance (PERF) as the output,
with Satisfaction (SATF), Communication (COMM), and
Efficiency (EFFC) serving as indirect factors that enhance
either input or output."

- "As illustrated in Figure 1, the proposed model generates
the following hypotheses:
H1: SATF positively affects ACTV
H2: SATF positively affects PERF
H3: COMM positively affects ACTV
H4: COMM positively affects PERF
H5: EFFC positively affects ACTV
H6: EFFC positively affects PERF
H7: ACTV positively affects PROD (Perceived
Productivity)
H8: PERF positively affects PROD"

- "This study's hypotheses regarding productivity
dimensions build upon established relationships documented
in earlier work. Job satisfaction and happiness influence
developer activity levels [24] [25], work quality [24], and
customer satisfaction [26]. Communication has a significant
impact on productivity at both individual and team levels,
enhancing decision-making, work quality, and defect
prevention [11]. Better communication boosts code output
[27], while meetings enhance team coordination and
information exchange [28]. Poor communication, however,
degrades work quality and performance [29]. Efficiency is
closely linked to managing disruptions and maintaining flow
states [20] [30] [31], with interruptions increasing errors [32]
and flow states boosting task completion [33]."

- "Participants were selected purposively from active
members of Indonesia’s professional software development
communities on LinkedIn, Facebook, and Discord."

- "To meet structural equation modeling (SEM)
requirements, we used the Inverse Square Root method (α =
0.05, |β| ≥ 0.2) [34], which indicated a minimum sample size
of 155. After removing outliers, the final dataset included 161
validated responses, yielding a 98% valid response rate."

- "B. Measurement Instrument
The study's dataset captures all five dimensions of the
SPACE Framework, with indicators adapted from established
literature. We measured each variable using a 5-point Likert
scale, where respondents selected 1 (strong disagreement)
through 5 (strong agreement) to indicate their level of
agreement with each statement. Table I presents the complete
mapping of framework dimensions to their specific
measurement indicators."

- "Analyses were conducted using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) implemented in the
seminr package for R (1,000 bootstrap iterations). This
method was selected based on its dual capabilities for complex
relationship modeling and predictive analysis, robustness to
non-normal distributions, and effectiveness with limited
samples [38]. The seminr environment ensured standardized
model specification and validation."

- "Participant demographics are detailed in Table II, covering
geographic location (domicile), gender, age range,
professional experience (years), development domain, and
team size. The sample consisted predominantly of male
respondents (94.41%, n=152), with female participants
representing 5.59% (n=9) of the total. This aligns with broader
trends in software engineering, where women remain
significantly underrepresented in technical roles [39]."

- "Hypothesis 1 (SATF → ACTV) is not supported with
almost no effect (β = -0.007) and a non-significant p-
value (p = 0.95).
Hypothesis 2 (SATF → PERF) is not supported with
weak and statistically insignificant relationship (β =
0.104, p = 0.16), as the confidence interval also
crosses zero ([-0.032, 0.259]).
Hypothesis 3 (COMM → ACTV) is supported; it
demonstrates a moderate and statistically significant
effect (β = 0.303, p = 0.006).
Hypothesis 4 (COMM → PERF) is strongly supported
with positive effect (β = 0.356, p < 0.001) and good
precision ([0.179, 0.536]).
Hypothesis 5 (EFFC → ACTV) is not supported with
nearly zero effect and no statistical significance (β =
0.012, p = 0.917).
Hypothesis 6 (EFFC → PERF) is supported, although
it gets a small positive effect yet is statistically
significant (β = 0.234, p = 0.008), suggesting a modest
yet meaningful relationship.
Hypothesis 7 (ACTV → PROD) is not supported by
shows no significant relationship (β = -0.049, p =
0.518).
Hypothesis 8 (PERF → PROD) is strongly supported;
it demonstrates a large and highly significant effect (β
= 0.602, p < 0.001), with a good confidence interval
([0.483, 0.705]), indicating a strong positive influence
on productivity."


- "The analysis reveals that Performance (PERF) plays a
pivotal role in determining productivity, demonstrating
significantly greater influence than other dimensions (β =
0.602, p < 0.001). The effects of other dimensions on
productivity are partially mediated through performance (0.17
≤ β ≤ 0.20), with the notable exception of Satisfaction
(SATF). The comparative effect size (f² = 0.483) between
PERF and Activity (ACTV) suggests that organizational
interventions targeting performance enhancement -
particularly through training and process optimization - yield
the most substantial productivity improvements."

- "Contrary to conventional expectations, Activity (ACTV)
demonstrates no statistically significant relationship with
productivity outcomes (β = -0.049, p = 0.518), nor does it
mediate other dimension-productivity relationships. While the
input-output model revealed a nominal trend where reduced
activity inputs weakly correlated with increased outputs, this
relationship was neither statistically significant nor practically
meaningful (effect size = 0.094). These findings suggest that
quantitative activity measures alone are unreliable
productivity indicators, aligning with growing evidence
favoring quality-based metrics [23]. "

- "Although development processes and tools are generally
consistent worldwide, work culture and industry trends vary
across countries. Future research could examine developer
productivity across different regions to assess the influence of
cultural and organizational differences on the applicability of
the SPACE Framework. Additionally, future studies could
broaden the scope by analyzing diverse respondent groups
across various roles and organizational sizes, providing a more
comprehensive understanding of productivity dynamics and
enhancing the generalizability of the findings."

- "This study reveals that software productivity depends
more on communication and output quality than on activity
metrics or job satisfaction."

- "While communication strongly
boosts both activity and performance, job satisfaction shows
no measurable impact."

- "Performance quality outweighs task
completion as a productivity driver, with efficiency mattering
only for performance. Future research should integrate
perceptual data with objective metrics (e.g., commit
frequency), establish standardized measurement protocols,
and include developers from diverse roles and cultural
backgrounds."
