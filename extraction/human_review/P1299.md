### Evidence (quotes with section/page) — required
- "Users were sensitive to the potential deskilling aspects of AI
assistance [42, 51]. R2.185 espoused a view that, “[I] [d]on’t really
like to use AI, makes people lazy and promotes to concept of not to
think.” R2.547 expressed the same concern, albeit more bluntly:
”I suspect we’re all going to get a lot stupider, doing a worse job of
maintaining larger amounts of worse code.” However, technological
acceptance takes time [14], and new technologies have a learning
curve before people can use them effectively; for LLMs, effective
use requires learning the art of prompt engineering [29, 32]. WCA
is no exception: “Very few people on my team have tried it, and
many aren’t sure how to engineer prompts to get effective answers
yet” (R1.92).
One final impact we noticed on the role of the developer is a
consequence of AI augmenting human abilities: when a developer
is more productive with AI, the expectations of their productivity
may also be increased. This sentiment was captured by P6, who
said, “Since our team is expected to use WCA our management is
expecting more work in one sprint as compared to before.”" —
