### Evidence (quotes with section/page) — required
- "We identify and analyze 23 developer bots that are deployed
across 13,000 repositories and assist about 6,000 developers daily
in their CI/CD software development workflows. We classify these
bots across five major categories: Config Violation, Security, Data-
privacy, Developer Productivity, and Code Quality. By conducting
interviews and surveys with bot developers and bot users and by
analyzing about half a million historical bot actions spanning over
one and a half years, we present software workflows that motivate
bot instrumentation, factors impacting their usefulness as perceived
by bot users, and challenges associated with their use" —

- "M5 DevProductivity. These bots improve developers’ produc-
tivity by automating some aspects of their development cycle. For
example, the "Flaky test bot" runs flaky tests (tests that fail inter-
mittently due to randomness in code) automatically across a set of
repositories and reports results. The "CodeReview" bot finds and
adds the right reviewers on pull-requests, and the "PRSignOff" bot
automatically completes routine pull request changes (for package
updates and configuration changes). Instead of generating sugges-
tions on pull-requests, they automatically finish tasks for develop-
ers."

- "To fill this gap, we study 23 developer bots that impact the work-
flows of more than 6,000 developers across 13,000 repositories in
Microsoft. These developer bots are deployed in continuous deliv-
ery environments [17] and act during different phases of software
development (e.g., work item creation and pull request review) to
assist software developers. We use semi-structured interviews with
bot developers and users, survey bot users, and analyze about one
and a half years of bot usage data (750,000 bot actions). Specifically,
our goals are summarized by the three research questions below:"

- "RQ1: What processes and needs motivate developers to instru-
ment bots in a large software engineering organization? What
challenges do they face in developing the bots?
RQ2: What is the experience of developers when using various
developer bots (benefits and challenges)?
RQ3: How do developers engage with different types of developer
bots?"

- "We analyzed the interviews using thematic analysis [7]. The
interview data consisted of about 18 hours of recordings and notes
taken during the interviews. The lead author went through the
interview transcriptions and notes over multiple passes and as-
signed open codes line by line to the transcripts. For bot developers,
the codes captured motivations for developing the bots (e.g., in-
crease test coverage, reduce review time) and challenges in bot
development (e.g., unclear metrics for success, no information on
engagement). For bot users, the codes captured the developer’s
opinions about bot use and associated problems (e.g., providing too
many suggestions helps find reviewers). The lead author discussed
the emerging codes with the study team members over multiple
passes and revised the codes, merging overlapping codes and re-
fining the coding to yield a set of final codes. The final codes were
then categorized into themes using affinity diagramming [20]. We
used Miro board 1 for the affinity diagramming step. We followed
the best practices in HCI to ensure reliability when coding and
discussing codes [30]. Figure 3 illustrates the codes that emerged
from the interviews."
